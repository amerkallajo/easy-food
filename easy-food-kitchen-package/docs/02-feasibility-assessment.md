# 2. Concept and location: feasibility assessment

Research date: **16 November 2026**. Sources are listed at the end. Anything marked *unverified* could not be confirmed from an accessible source.

## Verdict: proceed with specific changes

The location and format are a reasonable fit. The risks are technical (exhaust, gas, power) and commercial (price acceptance for a new category), and both can be tested cheaply before money is spent on equipment.

Changes that the verdict depends on:

1. **Low-grease cooking only.** Griddle, pots and rice cookers. No deep fryer, no charcoal, no shawarma spit. The only visible exhaust route is the street facade under occupied floors; a fryer or charcoal makes that hard to get accepted and unpleasant for neighbours.
2. **Cook on LPG, refrigerate on battery backup.** Grid supply in Damascus is still rationed and uneven.
3. **Launch with three lines** (bowls, wraps, salads) plus protein balls. Pasta in phase 2. Brownies bought in or baked off site.
4. **Price wraps close to shawarma and sell bowls as the step-up.** A bowl at about 1.7x the price of a shawarma sandwich is the untested part of the plan.
5. **Run a paid demand test before fit-out** (below).

## Will this audience buy it?

Evidence for:
- A city-centre daytime population with few quick lunch options that are not fried or bread-heavy.
- Health retail and several gyms nearby.
- Wraps and rice-with-chicken are familiar formats. Only the packaging and the "healthy" framing are new.

Evidence against:
- Purchasing power is tight. A national "shawarma index" put the Damascus average sandwich at 308 new SYP in September 2026 and noted that the minimum wage buys about 49 sandwiches a month.
- Red-meat prices rose about 30% in early 2026; beef dishes will be expensive to offer.

Conclusion: the wrap can compete on price with shawarma. The bowl is a premium product for a narrower group. Design the menu so the business works on wraps alone and bowls improve it.

## Exterior ordering and delivery pickup

- The existing glazing (about 1.7 m wide) converts to a two-sash service window: order and pay on the left, collect on the right. The counter sits about 1.05 m above the sidewalk because the floor is one step up.
- The sidewalk is wide enough for a short queue while leaving a pedestrian path, but it is public land. The cafe's stools show informal tolerance, not a right. Confirm with the municipality what may stand on the sidewalk.
- Delivery riders collect at the right-hand sash by order number and wait kerb-side, away from the order queue. BeeOrder operates in Damascus; its restaurant commission is **unverified**. Take orders on your own website and WhatsApp from day one so the commission is optional.

## Operating constraints with evidence

| Constraint | Evidence | Design response |

| Electricity supply | Reports through 2026 range from 3 h on / 3 h off in parts of Damascus to ministry claims of up to 20 h a day; a plant fire in October 2026 increased rationing. | Battery and inverter sized for refrigeration, hood fan, lights and POS. Cooking on LPG. |
| Electricity price | Tariff of 1,400-1,700 old SYP per kWh for small businesses and non-rationed users (14-17 new SYP, roughly $0.09-0.11). | About $110 a month at 1,000 kWh. Which tier applies to this meter is unverified. |

| Exchange rate | 16 Nov 2026: about 150 new SYP per USD on the parallel market, 122.5 official. | Budget in USD, price in SYP, review prices monthly. |
| Water | No evidence gathered for this building. | Ask your father about pressure and cut-offs; allow for a tank and pump. |
| Kitchen exhaust | No existing route visible. | The single largest approval risk. Get the building owner and municipality position before buying a hood. |

## Strengths and weaknesses of a 14 m² kitchen

Strengths: low rent exposure, three staff can run it, short walking distances, the whole room is visible so supervision is easy, small fit-out cost.

Weaknesses: no room for a second cook line, so hourly output is capped; one day of cold storage at high volume, so daily deliveries are needed; heat build-up; no staff area; raw and ready-to-eat separation depends on discipline and timing because space cannot provide it; one door serves staff and goods.

## Cheapest useful tests, in order

| # | Test | Cost | What it answers |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Footfall count.** Stand outside for 30 minutes at 08:00, 13:00, 15:00 and 20:00 on a weekday and a Saturday. Count passers-by and note who they are. | Free | Is there a lunch crowd, and who is it? |
| 2 | **Competitor price walk.** Photograph menus at the ten nearest fast-food shops. | Free | Where wrap and bowl prices must sit |
| 3 | **Site survey.** Tape-measure the room and confirmed rear-left WC; locate water, drain, meter and any rear shaft. Ask the building owner about a facade exhaust. | Free | Whether Layout A is buildable |
| 4 | **Recipe costing.** Cook each launch dish at home, weigh every ingredient, cost it at this week's market prices. | About $50 | Real food cost per dish |
| 5 | **Pre-order pop-up from MarCoffee.** For two to three weeks, sell 30-50 pre-ordered wraps and bowls a day, cooked in a licensed kitchen or at home where permitted, collected from the cafe at lunch. Promote with your own photography. | About $300-500 | Price acceptance, repeat rate, best sellers |
| 6 | **Office and gym pre-sales.** Offer a 10-meal card to three offices and two gyms. | Free | Whether repeat demand exists before opening |

Decision rule for test 5: if at least 40% of first-time buyers order again within two weeks and bowls are at least a third of sales at the target price, proceed to fit-out. If wraps sell but bowls do not, proceed with a wrap-led menu. If neither repeats, reconsider.
