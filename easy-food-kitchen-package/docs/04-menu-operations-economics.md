## Batch versus to order

| Component | Method | When | Holding |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rice, bulgur | Rice cookers, 2 batches staggered | Before each peak | Hot, bain-marie, above 63 °C, discard after 4 h |
| Chicken, beef | Marinated the evening before; **griddled in small batches every 10-15 minutes through the peak** | Continuous | Hot, bain-marie, 30-40 minutes maximum for quality |
| Grilled vegetables | Griddle, batch | Before peak | Hot or ambient for 2 h |
| Raw vegetables | Washed, cut, spun dry | Morning | Cold, refrigerated counter wells, below 5 °C |
| Sauces | Made daily | Morning | Cold, squeeze bottles in the counter |
| Wrap | Assembled and pressed 40-60 s on the griddle | To order | None |
| Bowl, salad | Assembled | To order | None |
| Protein balls | Rolled, boxed | Afternoon lull | Cold, 3 days |

Nothing is cooked from raw to order. That is what makes a three-person, 14 m² kitchen fast.

## Storage plan

| Store | Contents | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Upright chiller A | Raw marinated chicken and beef on the bottom shelves in lidded tubs; dairy and sauces above | Raw always below ready-to-eat |
| Upright chiller B | Washed and cut vegetables, back-up sauce, protein balls, drinks stock | Ready-to-eat only |
| Refrigerated prep counter | Service stock for the current peak | Refilled from chiller B, never topped up on old product |
| Dry shelving and high shelf | Rice, bulgur, oil, spices; packaging cartons at high level | Nothing on the floor |

At 150 orders a day this is roughly one day of fresh stock. Plan on **daily** vegetable and bread delivery and chicken every one to two days.

## Assembly and packing sequence

1. Cashier takes the order and payment at window 1. Ticket prints at the assembly station.
2. Assembler: base from the bain-marie, protein from the bain-marie, vegetables and sauce from the cold wells. Bowl target 45-60 seconds.
3. Wraps go to the cook for a 40-60 second press, then back.
4. Packer (the cashier between orders, or a fourth person at peak) lids, labels with the order number, bags, adds cutlery.
5. Hand-off at window 2 by order number.

## Staffing

| Role | Position | Does |
|---|---|---|
| S1 Cashier / packer | Front counter | Orders, payment, packing, hand-off, delivery riders |
| S2 Assembler | Refrigerated counter and bain-marie | Builds bowls, salads, wraps; refills wells |
| S3 Cook / shift lead | Griddle, range, rice | Batch-cooks protein and grains, presses wraps, food safety checks |
| S4 Prep / wash (part-time, mornings; fourth pair of hands at peak) | Back prep and sink | Washing, cutting, marinating, dishes, deep clean |

Minimum to trade: **three people at peak, two off-peak**. You or a trusted manager should be S1 for the first months.

## Bottlenecks

1. **Order window.** One cashier handles about 60-90 orders an hour. Pre-paid web and WhatsApp orders skip this.
2. **Griddle.** A 60 cm plate cooks about 10-12 chicken portions per 7-8 minute batch, so roughly 75-90 portions an hour with a cook who does nothing else. Wrap pressing competes for the same plate.
3. **Bain-marie volume.** Four pans. Rice needs two.
4. **Aisle.** 1.39 m. Two people work back to back and a third can pass behind them, but not with a wide tray during the rush.
5. **Cold stock.** The counter holds about one peak; refilling from the upright chiller opposite blocks the aisle for a moment.

## Service capacity

| Staffing | Sustained peak rate | Assumption |
|---|---|---|
| 3 people | **35-50 orders an hour** | Everything batch-cooked and held before the peak; 60% bowls and salads, 40% wraps |
| 4 people (dedicated cashier) | **50-65 orders an hour** | Cook does only cooking; pre-paid orders at least 30% |
| Daily, 3-4 people | **About 120-180 orders** | 2.5 peak hours plus 6-7 off-peak hours at 10-15 an hour; limited by cold storage and daily delivery |

These are planning ranges, not measurements. Time a real service during the pop-up test.

## Unit economics

### Per-dish contribution (all inputs are estimates to be replaced)

Assumed ingredient prices: chicken breast $5.00/kg (reports for 2026 range from about 500 to 1,100 new SYP/kg, so $3.30-7.30); lean beef $11/kg (veal cuts were reported at 1,600 new SYP/kg in February 2026); rice $1.50/kg; vegetables $0.60/kg average.

| | Chicken wrap | Chicken bowl | Beef bowl |
|---|---|---|---|
| Test price | 375 SYP ($2.50) | 525 SYP ($3.50) | 630 SYP ($4.20) |
| Protein (raw weight) | 120 g: $0.60 | 180 g: $0.90 | 150 g: $1.65 |
| Base | bread $0.10 | rice $0.14 | rice $0.14 |
| Vegetables | $0.04 | $0.09 | $0.09 |

| **Ingredient cost** | **$0.89 (36%)** | **$1.38 (39%)** | **$2.13 (51%)** |
| Packaging | $0.10 | $0.30 | $0.30 |
| Waste allowance (4% of ingredients) | $0.04 | $0.06 | $0.09 |
| **Contribution per item** | **$1.47** | **$1.76** | **$1.68** |

Reference point: the average Damascus shawarma sandwich was 308 SYP ($2.05) in September 2026.

The beef bowl is the weak item at these prices: half the price goes on ingredients. Either price it near 700 SYP, cut the portion to 120 g, or use kofta from mince.

### Break-even (26 trading days a month)

| Case | Orders a month | Orders a day |
|---|---|---|
| Cash break-even: bills and wages only, rent and your pay ignored | 821 | **32** |
| **Operating break-even**: all costs including market rent and owner pay, walk-up only | 1,449 | **56** |
| Operating break-even with 30% of orders through a 20%-commission app | 1,644 | **63** |

Operating break-even is 56-63 orders a day, about a third to a half of what the kitchen can produce. That is a workable margin of safety, provided the demand exists.

### Recovering the investment

Operating break-even only stops losses. Paying back the fit-out needs volume above it. Using a mid-range investment of $25,000 (see the budget) and walk-up contribution:

| Orders a day | Monthly surplus after all fixed costs | Payback |
|---|---|---|
| 60 | $194 | More than 10 years: not viable |
| 80 | $1,104 | About 23 months |
| 100 | $2,014 | About 12 months |
| 130 | $3,379 | About 7 months |

The business needs to **average around 100 orders a day** to be worth the investment. The pop-up test should tell you whether that is plausible. If you ignore rent and your own pay, the same figures look far better, which is exactly why they should not be ignored.

## What makes the numbers better

- Pre-paid orders through your own site and WhatsApp: no commission and no queue.
- Office meal cards (10 meals pre-paid): cash up front and predictable volume.
- Bites and drinks at the window: about $0.55 extra contribution on a quarter of orders.
- A whole-grain base: bulgur costs less than rice.
