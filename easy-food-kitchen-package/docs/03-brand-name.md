# 3. Name and brand direction

## Recommendation: **Easy Food**, written as the existing "/// easy FOOD" wordmark, with **إيزي فود** in Arabic

| Criterion | Easy Food | EZ Food |
|---|---|---|
| Pronunciation | Said the same way by everyone. | The pun only works if Z is read "zee". In Syria the letter is usually read "zed", so many people will say "E-Zed Food". |
| Arabic | إيزي فود reads naturally and matches how people already say "easy". | Either إي زد فود (awkward) or إيزي فود, which is just Easy Food again, so the Latin spelling adds nothing. |
| Memorability | Plain, a little generic. | Looks more like a logo, but is harder to say aloud and to pass on by word of mouth. |
| Signage | Four lowercase letters, "easy", read well at distance; already designed. | Two capitals are very compact, but look like an abbreviation or a tech brand. |
| Search | Generic phrase; hard to rank for on its own. | Shorter but ambiguous; people will type "easy food" anyway. |
| Positioning | Says simple, quick, approachable. It does not say healthy, so the descriptor line must. | Reads as cheap and fast. Works against a healthy positioning. |
| Growth | Sub-brands already work: Easy Rice, Easy Wrap, Easy Salad, Easy Bites. | "EZ Rice", "EZ Wrap" are possible but lose the Arabic link. |

Why Easy Food wins: word of mouth in Damascus will happen in Arabic, and only "easy" survives being spoken. The existing identity is already built on it.

## Arabic presentation

- Name: **إيزي فود** (with hamza under the alef; avoid the Egyptian spelling إيزى).
- Descriptor under the name, always present on the fascia and social profiles: **أكلك الصحي، بكل بساطة**. This carries the health claim the name does not.
- Fascia order: Latin wordmark on the left, Arabic on the right, equal visual weight. This is how the sign is drawn in the 3D model.

## Dealing with the generic name

Because the phrase is generic, make these three things do the distinguishing:

1. The **three-slash symbol** in lime on forest green. Use it alone on the projecting sign, bags and stickers.
2. A specific handle and search phrase everywhere: for example `easyfood.sy` or `easyfood.dam` plus "إيزي فود دمشق". *Availability not checked.*
3. A Google Business Profile at this address under the Arabic and Latin names on opening day. The MarCoffee listing is unclaimed; ask Google to mark it closed or moved when the cafe stops trading.

## Using the existing identity

Use the **v2 green** identity in `easy-food-identity-v2-green/`. Green on cream reads as fresh food; the cobalt version does not.

Recommended changes:
- Add the Arabic name as a locked-up logo version. At present Arabic appears only as a supporting line.
- Lime (#DDF45B) text on cream fails contrast. Use lime only on forest green or as a block colour.
- Add an "Easy Bites" icon for protein balls and brownies to match the rice, wrap and salad icons.
- The brandboard shows indoor seating and a wide shopfront. Replace it with the exterior render from this package when presenting the real shop.

## What "healthy" has to mean

The name does not support a health claim. These commitments do, and each one is checkable:

1. Protein is grilled on a flat-top with measured oil. Nothing is deep-fried.
2. Every bowl is built to a fixed ratio by weight: about 130 g cooked protein (180 g raw), 180 g cooked grain, 150 g vegetables.
3. A whole-grain base is always offered at the same price: bulgur or freekeh alongside white rice. Both are local and cheap.
4. Sauces are yogurt, tahini, lemon or tomato based and portioned with a 30 g ladle or cup.
5. Calories and protein per item are printed on the menu once recipes are fixed and weighed.
6. Protein balls and brownies state their sugar source and amount. If a brownie has as much sugar as a normal one, do not call it healthy; call it "date and cocoa brownie".

Avoid medical words such as "diet", "weight loss" or "sugar-free" unless the recipe and local advertising rules support them.
